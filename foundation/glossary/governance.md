# Governance

## Definition

The organizational capability that defines who has authority, what is canonical, how exceptions are recorded, and how change propagates across a design system.

## Why It Matters

Consistency without governance is voluntary. Voluntary consistency does not survive scale, turnover, or acquisitions. Governance is how intent becomes enforceable without becoming arbitrary gatekeeping.

## Relationships

- **design-system** — governance is constitutive, not optional
- **decision** — governance produces and protects decisions
- **lifecycle** — governance authorizes state transitions
- **principle** — governance operationalizes principles
- **interpretive-debt** — weak governance allows debt to accumulate unnoticed

## Common Misinterpretations

- Reducing governance to review boards or approval workflows only
- Treating governance as bureaucracy to remove when delivery pressure rises
- Confusing governance (capability) with a governance document (artifact)

## Genshi Interpretation

Governance is **first-class** (Principle 02). It enables speed when rules are explicit. The metamodel attaches decision rights to entities; the glossary defines governance as the capability those rights serve.
